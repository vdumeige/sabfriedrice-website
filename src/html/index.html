Two kitchens, one table

Thai and Lao food grew up next to each other, and we cook both. That means coconut curries and pad thai on the same menu as laab, nam khao and papaya salad — the sour, herby, chilli-forward side of the border that most places leave off.

Everything is cooked to order, so the heat is yours to choose. Ask for it mild and we will make it mild; ask for it the way we eat it and we will do that too.

Start with the nam khao

Crispy rice broken over cured pork with lime, peanuts and a fistful of herbs, wrapped in lettuce at the table. It is the dish we hand to anyone who says they have never eaten Lao food before.

What people order

$12.99

An assortment of our appetisers on one plate, with the dipping sauces to match. The easiest way to try several things at once.

Green Curry

$11.99

A milder Thai curry built on green curry paste, coconut milk, Thai eggplant and basil. Choose your protein and your heat.

Crispy fried rice tossed with cured pork, peanuts, lime and herbs. Wrap it in the lettuce leaves it comes with.

Laab

$12.99

The Lao national dish: minced chicken, pork or beef with lime, toasted rice powder, chilli and a great deal of mint.

Tom Yum Combination Seafood

$15.99

Hot-and-sour broth with lemongrass, galangal and kaffir lime, loaded with shrimp, squid and fish. Served in a hot pot.

Pad Ke Mow

$12.99

Drunken noodles — wide rice noodles seared with basil, chilli and vegetables. One of the hotter things on the menu.
